## 30. How do data, parameters, and compute interact?

### Answer

The capability and loss of a Large Language Model are strictly governed by the empirical interplay between three foundational pillars:
1. **Compute ($C$):** The total number of floating-point operations (FLOPs) available for training.
2. **Parameters ($N$):** The number of learnable non-embedding weights in the neural network architecture.
3. **Data ($D$):** The total number of unique training tokens ingested.

The mathematical bridge connecting these three variables is the fundamental **Compute Budget Equation**:

$$C \approx 6 \cdot N \cdot D$$

```text
Where does the factor of 6 come from?
- Forward Pass:  ~2 FLOPs per parameter per token (1 multiply-accumulate = 1 mult + 1 add).
- Backward Pass: ~4 FLOPs per parameter per token (computing gradients with respect to activations 
                 and weights takes twice the computation of the forward pass).
- Total:         2 + 4 = 6 FLOPs / (parameter · token)
```

### Kaplan et al. (2020) vs. Chinchilla Scaling Laws (Hoffmann et al., 2022)

In 2020, OpenAI published seminal work by Jared Kaplan et al., claiming that parameter count should scale much faster than dataset size ($N \propto C^{0.73}$ vs. $D \propto C^{0.27}$). This led the industry to construct massive, data-starved models such as GPT-3 (175B on 300B tokens) and Gopher (280B on 300B tokens).

In 2022, DeepMind's Hoffmann et al. identified a critical flaw in Kaplan's learning rate schedule and established the **Chinchilla Scaling Laws**:
- When given a fixed compute budget $C$, **parameters ($N$) and training tokens ($D$) should be scaled in equal proportion**:
  $$N \propto C^{0.5} \quad \text{and} \quad D \propto C^{0.5}$$
- **The Golden Ratio:** For compute-optimal training, a model should be trained on approximately **20 tokens per parameter**:
  $$D_{\text{optimal}} \approx 20 \cdot N$$

Under Chinchilla laws, DeepMind trained **Chinchilla (70B parameters on 1.4 trillion tokens)**. Despite having 60% fewer parameters than GPT-3 (175B), Chinchilla outperformed GPT-3, Gopher, and Jurassic-1 across virtually every academic benchmark while using identical training compute.

---

### The Power-Law Loss Formulation

Hoffmann et al. modeled cross-entropy test loss $L(N, D)$ as a bivariate power law:

$$L(N, D) = E + \frac{A}{N^\alpha} + \frac{B}{D^\beta}$$

Where:
- $E = 1.69$: The irreducible entropy of natural human language (the theoretical lower bound).
- $A / N^\alpha$: The loss penalty due to limited model capacity ($\alpha \approx 0.34, A \approx 406.4$).
- $B / D^\beta$: The loss penalty due to limited training data ($\beta \approx 0.28, B \approx 410.7$).

If you scale parameters to infinity without scaling data, performance saturates at $E + B/D^\beta$. Conversely, if you feed trillions of tokens to a tiny model, performance saturates at $E + A/N^\alpha$.

---

### Compute-Optimal vs. Inference-Optimal Scaling

While Chinchilla established the optimal tradeoff for **training compute**, modern production engineering operates under **Inference-Optimal Scaling** (pioneered by Meta's LLaMA project):

| Dimension | Chinchilla Compute-Optimal | Modern Inference-Optimal (LLaMA-3) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Optimization Target** | Minimizes total FLOPs spent during training to achieve target loss. | Minimizes total end-to-end cost: $(\text{Training Cost}) + (\text{Inference Cost} \times \text{Queries})$. |
| **Token-to-Param Ratio** | $\approx 20 \text{ tokens per parameter}$ | $\approx 200 \text{ to } 2,000 \text{ tokens per parameter}$ |
| **Concrete Example** | Chinchilla: 70B params on 1.4T tokens ($20:1$). | LLaMA-3: 8B params on 15T tokens ($1,875:1$). |
| **Training Budget** | Optimal FLOP expenditure for pretraining. | "Sub-optimal" training FLOPs (intentionally overtrained). |
| **Deployment Efficiency** | High serving cost: requires multiple GPUs and massive KV cache memory. | Ultra-low serving cost: fits on a single commodity GPU; faster token-per-second generation. |

```text
The Economic Tradeoff:
Pretraining is paid ONCE by the AI lab.
Inference is paid MILLIONS OF TIMES per day by users and production APIs.
Spending $2M extra in pretraining to overtrain an 8B model saves $50M+ in cloud serving costs!
```

---

### Key Point

> **Training compute follows $C \approx 6ND$. Chinchilla scaling established that compute-optimal training requires scaling parameters and tokens equally at a ratio of ~20 tokens per parameter. However, production systems intentionally "overtrain" smaller models (e.g., LLaMA-3 8B on 15T tokens) to achieve inference optimality and minimize serving latency and VRAM costs.**

### Interview Answer

> "The interaction between data ($D$), parameters ($N$), and compute ($C$) is governed by $C \approx 6ND$ FLOPs. 
> 
> Historically, Kaplan et al. advocated scaling parameters faster than tokens. DeepMind's Chinchilla paper corrected this, proving that for compute-optimal training, parameters and tokens should scale equally at roughly 20 tokens per parameter (demonstrated by Chinchilla-70B outperforming the undertrained GPT-3 175B).
> 
> In modern production, the industry has shifted from compute-optimality to **inference-optimality**. Models like LLaMA-3 (8B parameters trained on 15 trillion tokens) intentionally overtrain far past the Chinchilla frontier. Because pretraining is a one-time fixed cost while inference is paid on every user query, overtraining smaller architectures yields massive long-term latency, memory, and serving cost savings."
