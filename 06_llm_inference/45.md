## 45. What does temperature do?

### Answer

**Temperature ($T$)** is a runtime sampling hyperparameter derived from statistical physics (the Boltzmann distribution) that scales the logit vector prior to softmax normalization, directly modulating the **entropy, randomness, and diversity** of the generated tokens.

Temperature does **not** change the internal weights or the relative ranking of tokens; rather, it controls **how sharply the model discriminates between high-scoring and low-scoring candidates**.

```text
                               The Effect of Temperature Scaling
                               
                                      Raw Logits z_i
                                            │
                                            ▼
                          Scale by Temperature: z_i / T
                                            │
                                            ▼
               Softmax: P(v_i) = exp(z_i / T) / Σ exp(z_j / T)
                                            │
         ┌──────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                  ▼                                  ▼
 Low Temp (T = 0.2)                 Neutral (T = 1.0)                 High Temp (T = 1.8)
 High Peak / Low Entropy            Original Model Distribution       Flat / High Entropy
 ┌──────────────────────┐           ┌──────────────────────┐          ┌──────────────────────┐
 │ █                    │           │ █                    │          │ █                    │
 │ █                    │           │ █   ▄                │          │ █   ▆   ▄            │
 │ █   ▂                │           │ █   █   ▂            │          │ █   █   █   ▄   ▂    │
 └──────────────────────┘           └──────────────────────┘          └──────────────────────┘
 Deterministic & Focused            Balanced Generation               Creative & Chaotic
```

### The Mathematical Formulation & Extreme Limits

The temperature-adjusted probability of token $i$ is defined as:

$$P(x_i) = \frac{\exp\left(\frac{z_i}{T}\right)}{\sum_{j=1}^{|V|} \exp\left(\frac{z_j}{T}\right)}$$

The mathematical behavior spans three distinct regimes:

#### 1. Limit as $T \to 0$ (Greedy Decoding / Determinism)
As $T$ approaches zero, the ratio $z_i / T$ magnifies the difference between the maximum logit and all other logits toward infinity:
$$\lim_{T \to 0^+} P(x_i) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } i = \arg\max_k z_k \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
The probability distribution collapses into a **Dirac delta function**. The model deterministically selects the single highest-probability token.

#### 2. Neutral Regime ($T = 1.0$)
When $T=1.0$, the equation simplifies to standard softmax. The model samples tokens according to the true probability distribution learned during pretraining.

#### 3. Limit as $T \to \infty$ (Uniform Random Chaos)
As $T$ approaches infinity, every logit divided by $T$ approaches zero:
$$\lim_{T \to \infty} \frac{z_i}{T} = 0 \implies \exp(0) = 1$$
$$P(x_i) = \frac{1}{\sum_{j=1}^{|V|} 1} = \frac{1}{|V|}$$
The distribution becomes **completely uniform**: every token in the 128,000-word vocabulary has an identical chance of being selected, resulting in pure, ungrounded gibberish.

### Worked Numerical Example

Consider a small vocabulary of 3 candidate tokens with raw logits:
$$\mathbf{z} = [\text{"Paris"}: 6.0, \quad \text{"Lyon"}: 4.0, \quad \text{"Mars"}: 1.0]$$

| Candidate Token | Raw Logit ($z_i$) | Prob at $T = 0.5$ (Low) | Prob at $T = 1.0$ (Neutral) | Prob at $T = 2.0$ (High) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **"Paris"** | $6.0$ | **$97.9\%$** | **$86.6\%$** | **$67.2\%$** |
| **"Lyon"** | $4.0$ | $2.0\%$ | $11.7\%$ | $24.7\%$ |
| **"Mars"** | $1.0$ | $0.1\%$ | $1.7\%$ | $8.1\%$ |

- At $T = 0.5$, `"Paris"` dominates overwhelmingly ($97.9\%$), suppressing lower-ranked alternatives.
- At $T = 2.0$, `"Mars"` sees its probability spike nearly $5\times$ (from $1.7\%$ to $8.1\%$), dramatically increasing the likelihood of an unexpected or hallucinated output.

### Production Recommended Settings

| Task / Domain | Recommended Temperature | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Code Generation & SQL** | $T = 0.0$ to $0.1$ | Syntax errors cannot be tolerated; requires deterministic logic. |
| **JSON Extraction / RAG QA** | $T = 0.0$ to $0.2$ | Answers must adhere strictly to schema and retrieved facts. |
| **Summarization & Translation**| $T = 0.3$ to $0.5$ | Balances faithful factual retention with fluent sentence flow. |
| **Creative Writing / Brainstorming**| $T = 0.7$ to $0.9$ | Encourages diverse vocabulary, figurative analogies, and novel ideas. |
| **Extreme Uncontrolled Generation**| $T \ge 1.3$ | Generally avoided in production; high risk of repetition loops or gibberish. |

---

### Key Point

> **Temperature scales the logit vector by dividing by scalar $T$ before the softmax step. Low temperature ($T < 1$) sharpens the probability peak toward the argmax for deterministic, factual outputs, while high temperature ($T > 1$) flattens the distribution to foster creative variability at the cost of hallucination risk.**

---

### Interview Answer

> "Temperature is a sampling hyperparameter that controls generation entropy by dividing the raw logit vector $\mathbf{z}$ by a scalar $T$ before evaluating softmax: $P(x_i) = \frac{\exp(z_i / T)}{\sum \exp(z_j / T)}$.
> 
> When $T < 1$, the mathematical distances between logits are magnified, sharpening the distribution peak toward the argmax. This makes generation deterministic, focused, and ideal for tasks requiring factual precision like SQL synthesis or structured JSON extraction. 
> 
> As $T$ approaches 0, it converges to greedy decoding. Conversely, when $T > 1$, logit differences are compressed, flattening the distribution toward uniform random noise, which increases creative diversity but elevates hallucination and incoherence risk."
