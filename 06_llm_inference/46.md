## 46. What is top-k sampling?

### Answer

**Top-K Sampling** (Fan et al., 2018) is a stochastic decoding and truncation strategy that restricts the sampling pool to the **$K$ most probable tokens** in the vocabulary, completely eliminating the long, improbable tail of candidate tokens before renormalizing the remaining probabilities.

By capping candidate selection to a strict integer count $K$, it prevents the model from sampling low-probability, nonsensical, or pathological words that could derail generation.

```text
                                The Top-K Sampling Process
                                
 Full Vocabulary (e.g., |V| = 128,000 tokens) sorted by probability:
 ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │ Rank 1:  "Paris"     (P = 0.62)                                             │ ◄── Included
 │ Rank 2:  "Rome"      (P = 0.18)                                             │ ◄── Included
 │ Rank 3:  "Berlin"    (P = 0.12)                                             │ ◄── Included (If K = 3)
 ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │ Rank 4:  "the"       (P = 0.04)  ──► MASKED TO -∞ (P = 0)                   │
 │ Rank 5:  "banana"    (P = 0.01)  ──► MASKED TO -∞ (P = 0)                   │
 │ ...                                                                         │
 │ Rank 128,000: "xyz"  (P ≈ 0)     ──► MASKED TO -∞ (P = 0)                   │
 └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                                       │
                                       ▼ Renormalize Top-K Subset
                        "Paris"  : 0.62 / (0.62+0.18+0.12) = 67.4%
                        "Rome"   : 0.18 / 0.92             = 19.6%
                        "Berlin" : 0.12 / 0.92             = 13.0%
```

---

### Step-by-Step Mathematical Algorithm

At generation step $t$:
1. Compute the raw logit vector $\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^{|V|}$.
2. Sort the vocabulary indices in descending order of their logits:
   $$z_{(1)} \ge z_{(2)} \ge \dots \ge z_{(|V|)}$$
3. Define the active candidate subset $V^{(K)}$ containing the top $K$ ranked indices:
   $$V^{(K)} = \{ (1), (2), \dots, (K) \}$$
4. Set the logits of all remaining tokens to negative infinity:
   $$z_i' = \begin{cases} z_i & \text{if } i \in V^{(K)} \\ -\infty & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
5. Apply softmax to obtain the **renormalized probability distribution**:
   $$P'(x_i) = \frac{\exp(z_i')}{\sum_{j \in V^{(K)}} \exp(z_j')}$$
6. Sample token $x_t$ from $P'$.

---

### The Fundamental Flaw of Top-K: Fixed-K Inflexibility

While Top-K eliminates the tail, its fatal flaw is that **$K$ is a constant integer, whereas language entropy is dynamic and context-dependent**.

Consider the two contrasting scenarios below:

```text
 Scenario A: Sharp Distribution (Low Entropy / Factual Recall)
 Prompt: "The capital of France is"
 Logits: ["Paris": 99.4%, "Lyon": 0.3%, "Marseille": 0.1%, "banana": 0.001%, ...]
 - If K = 50: The model retains 48 completely incorrect tokens in the pool!
   A bad random sample could pick "banana", producing a catastrophic hallucination.
 
 Scenario B: Flat Distribution (High Entropy / Creative Opening)
 Prompt: "Once upon a"
 Logits: ["time": 12%, "midnight": 10%, "hill": 9%, "cold": 8%, "sunny": 8%, ..., "distant": 2%]
 - If K = 5: Top-K prematurely cuts off dozens of rich, contextually valid narrative words!
```

Because natural language constantly shifts between high confidence and broad possibility, a fixed $K$ cannot adapt to varying levels of contextual uncertainty. This direct limitation inspired the invention of **Top-P (Nucleus) Sampling**.

---

### Top-K in Combination with Temperature

In production inference engines (e.g., vLLM, HuggingFace TGI), Top-K is executed in sequence with temperature:

```text
 [Raw Logits z] ──► [Temperature Scaling: z / T] ──► [Top-K Truncation] ──► [Softmax] ──► [Sample]
```

- When $K = 1$: Top-K reduces mathematically to pure **Greedy Decoding**, regardless of temperature.
- Typical production values for $K$ range between $20$ and $50$.

---

### Key Point

> **Top-K sampling restricts candidate selection to the top $K$ highest-probability tokens and renormalizes the remaining distribution. While it successfully eliminates the improbable tail of the vocabulary, its static size cannot adapt dynamically to varying language entropy—retaining bad tokens when confidence is high and cutting off valid tokens when confidence is low.**

### Interview Answer

> "Top-K sampling is a truncation decoding strategy that sorts vocabulary tokens by logit score, retains only the top $K$ highest-scoring tokens, masks the remaining vocabulary to $-\infty$, and renormalizes the distribution via softmax before sampling.
> 
> The primary benefit is preventing the generation of bizarre or out-of-context tokens residing in the long tail of the vocabulary. 
> 
> However, Top-K's critical architectural flaw is its static threshold: language entropy is dynamic. When predicting an unambiguous fact like 'The capital of France is...', a fixed $K=40$ dangerously retains dozens of incorrect tokens; conversely, in high-entropy contexts like 'Once upon a...', $K=40$ arbitrarily truncates valid words. This limitation led directly to the adoption of Top-P (nucleus) sampling."
